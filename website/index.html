For grown-ups

Building reading skills, one step at a time

A simple, joyful routine from the front of the book. Use it with any letter, go at your child's pace and enjoy the adventure.

  1. Name the letterShow the uppercase and lowercase letter and say its name. This is A. This is a.
  2. Say the soundA says /ă/, as in Alana and ant.
  3. Listen for itDo you hear /ă/ at the beginning of apple?
  4. Find itSearch the picture for things that begin with the sound.
  5. Notice what's differentFind something that begins with a different sound. What do you hear?
  6. Think of moreCan you think of another word that begins with /ă/?
  7. Trace & writeTrace the letters with a finger. Write them when you're ready.
  8. Blend soundsWhen ready, blend sounds to read simple words: m–a–p → map.

Remember

You don't need to do every step every time. Follow your child's interest, celebrate effort, and remember that a few happy minutes together are worth more than finishing a whole lesson.

About us

Made to be read again (and again)

Sounds and Stories Press is a small, family-run imprint in Laguna Beach, California. We make books that help little ones hear the sounds of language and fall in love with stories. On every page there's a way for a child to succeed, even before they can read the words: point to a picture, say its name, and enjoy the moment together. Along the way, children learn that books are a safe and happy place to be.

Reading should feel more like a game than a test.From "A Note for Grown-Ups" in The Amazing Alphabet Adventure

Meet the author

Dawn Winson

Dawn Winson created The Amazing Alphabet Adventure for two very special little readers: her granddaughters, Alana and Raeya. What began as a way to make learning to read fun grew into a world of lovable characters, stories, sounds, games and discoveries.

Through Sounds & Stories Press, Dawn hopes to help children build early reading skills through the thing they already know how to do best: play.

Look for two friends named after the girls who inspired it all, and one named for the grandma they call Momo.
